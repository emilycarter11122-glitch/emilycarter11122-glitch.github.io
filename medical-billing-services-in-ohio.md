## A 5-Step Checklist for Choosing Medical Billing Services in Ohio
### Define what you need.
List your current problems: late claim submission, repeated denials, unpaid claims, unposted payments. Decide what stays in-house and what moves out. Write down who owns each task today.

### Check fit with your practice and payers. 
Ask about experience with therapy, psychiatry, and group practice billing. Then ask which payers they bill, including Ohio Medicaid plans and commercial insurers, if they apply to you. A payer is the insurance plan that pays the claim.

### Ask how the work gets done.
Find out who submits claims, who fixes rejections, who follows up on denials, and who posts payments. Ask who your day-to-day contact is. A claim is the bill sent to a payer. A rejection usually stops before the payer processes it, often at the clearinghouse, a service that checks claims before passing them on. A denial is processed but not paid. Details vary by payer and clearinghouse.

### Review reporting and transparency. 
Ask for sample rejection, denial, and aging reports. Aging shows how long accounts receivable (AR)  money owed to you has gone unpaid. Ask how often reports arrive and whether you can see your own claim data. Also ask how posting is checked. Posting means matching remittances and the payer’s payment explanations to your records. A missed posting can make a paid claim look open.

### Read the contract, fees, and data terms.
Ask how fees are explained, how long the contract runs, how you can exit, and how your data comes back. Ask whether a business associate agreement (BAA) is in place — a written agreement on how protected patient information is handled.

Practical tip: Give each vendor the same test case — a claim denied for missing authorization, or a claim sent to the wrong payer. Ask how they’d catch it, fix it, and report it. Their answers show how they really work.

Payer rules, filing windows, and processing times differ, so check them with each payer.

### Red Flags to Watch For

Each flag below comes with what it looks like and a first action.

### Vague answers on denials. 
Nobody can say who works a denied claim. First action: ask for the workflow in writing.

### No useful sample reports. 
Reports exist but don’t show where claims are stuck. First action: request sample rejection, denial, and aging reports.
### Unclear fees. 
Charges sound simple, but extras show up later. First action: ask for a written fee breakdown.
### Thin behavioral health or payer experience. 
Answers stay general. First action: ask which practice types and payers they handle today.
### No BAA or hard exit terms. 
Data access is unclear, or leaving looks costly. First action: ask for the BAA and contract terms in writing before signing anything.

### Compare Options Side by Side

Use a simple scorecard. Label vendors Option A, Option B, and Option C. Add rows for services covered, payers handled, reporting, fees explained, contract and exit terms, contact person, and notes. Mark each answer as clear, vague, or missing.

If two or three options give vague answers on the same item, treat it as a pattern. Push for a written answer.

### Outside Support or an In-House Fix?
Outside support may fit if claims are tracked informally or by memory. It may also fit if the same denials keep repeating, staff are buried in rework and payer follow-up, or no report shows where claims are stuck.

An in-house fix may be enough when one clear cause is behind the trouble. A front desk that keeps mistyping member IDs is one example. A second check at registration can fix that. So can a reminder to sign notes on time, if late notes are holding claims back.

### Conclusion

Choosing a billing partner gets easier when you start with your own problems. Before you compare medical billing services in Ohio, write down your three biggest billing problems and who owns each one. Then ask every vendor the same questions and keep the answers in writing. ### Frequently Asked Questions

**Should I outsource all my billing or just part?**
Either can work. Some practices move only denial follow-up or payment posting. Decide after you list your problems and who owns each task.

**What happens to open claims when I switch?**
Ask for a written transition plan. It should say who works claims already submitted, who handles older unpaid ones, and when the handoff happens.

**How do I check the work after hiring?**
Review reports on a set schedule. Spot-check a few paid claims against remittances. Keep a log of repeat problems with the same payer or stage.
